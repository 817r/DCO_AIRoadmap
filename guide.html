Getting started

DCO_AI replaces how vanilla AI thinks. Any AI already placed in a scenario picks up the new behaviour — you don't rebuild missions or place special units.

  1. Subscribe to DCO_AI Reforger on the Arma Reforger Workshop.
  2. Enable it as a mod for your scenario, or add it to your server's mod list.
  3. Load any scenario with AI.Soldiers now suppress, dodge, fight from cover, share contacts and react as a group.

Pick your seat

PlayerCommand the AI through your group command menu: report enemies, request support, call fire missions.
Game MasterEditing a running session. Place a commander, assign groups and drag the sliders live — nothing is saved.
Workbench mission makerBuilding a scenario in the World Editor. Place the prefabs and set component attributes that ship with the mission.
Server ownerTune everything from JSON files in the profile folder, without opening an editor at all.
Game Master and Workbench are not the same job. Game Master changes last until the session ends; Workbench changes are saved into the mission. They reach almost the same settings — see Workbench vs Game Master.

Five minutes to two AI sides fighting

All of this is done inside Game Master — no Workbench needed.

  1. Place an AI Commander for each AI side.Set its Faction Key and a short Commander UID, and pick a mode. Without a faction it refuses every group.
  2. Place two or more AI Commander Objectives.Name, radius (a town 100–150 m, a farm 40–60 m) and Controlled By for whoever starts with it. Leave that empty for neutral.
  3. Place AI groups near their side and set each group's Commander.A group without one still fights with DCO behaviour, it just never gets orders. A spawner does this step for you.
  4. Optional: a Spawner per side, a Logistics Hub, and a mortar team.A mortar plus a group of up to 4 whose leader stands within 40 m of it becomes a mortar crew by itself, once it has a commander.
  5. Press F2 for DCO Settings and load a preset.Recommended to start; Players Defend or Players Attack for coop.
  6. Unpause.The commander thinks for the first time after about a minute, then every 30 s.
Version note. Anything marked Dev build here is written but not in the public Workshop version yet — check the roadmap for what's live.

For players

You talk to the AI commander through the same group command menu you already use to order your own squad. Aim at a position or an enemy, open the menu and pick a DCO command. Everything you send goes to the commander of your faction, which decides what to do with it.

Reporting what you see

Contact reports are how the commander learns about the enemy. It reacts to reports from its own units and from players — an unreported enemy might as well not exist.

CommandReports
Report teamAbout 4 infantry at the marked spot
Report squadAbout 8 infantry
Report platoonAbout 24 infantry
Report vehicleA vehicle contact
Report armorAn armored contact, flagged as an anti-armor threat
Distance costs accuracy. A report carries position error that grows with how far you were from what you saw — roughly 12 m of error for every 100 m of distance. That error decides how precisely artillery can be aimed later, so report from as close as you safely can.

Asking for support

RequestWhat the commander does
SuppressPuts fire on the marked position so you can move
SupportLinks a nearby element to your squad to support your fight
Flank left / rightSends an element around the side you picked
ArmorRequests armored support at the marked position
TransportSends a transport to pick you up — accept the ride when it arrives
ExtractPulls your element out of the area
MEDEVACSends a casualty evacuation

Taking a role

You can also volunteer your squad for a job: assault, fix (hold the enemy in place), block or recon — or ask the commander to give you a different task. The commander sends tasks back to you with a proper task marker and a radio call.

The commander remembers you. It tracks whether tasks you accept get done. Finishing tasks earns a period where your requests are favoured; repeatedly abandoning them puts your requests on cooldown for a while.

Every standing order can be called off: cancel fire support, cancel the support element, cancel transport, or cancel everything at once.

Calling fire missions v0.4.142

Mark a target, pick the shell type and how many rounds you want. The commander has to approve it, and may approve fewer rounds than you asked for.

TypeRoundsNotes
HE1, 3, 5, 8Needs an observed target — an unobserved area is refused
Smoke1, 3, 5Falls back to a rough area mission if nobody has eyes on
Illumination1, 2, 3Same area fallback; useful before a night assault

Why a mission gets cut or refused

ReasonWhat happens
No observationHE is refused; smoke and illumination drop to an imprecise area mission
Friendlies near the targetHE is refused outright
Not enough tubesRounds cut to the number of guns available
Queue busyCut to about half of what you asked for
Cautious commanderHE capped at 5 rounds
Danger closeHE capped at 3 rounds when you're near the impact area

After an approved mission your group is on cooldown: about 30s + 10s per round for smoke and illumination, and 60s + 20s per round for HE. So eight rounds of HE lock your group out for roughly three and a half minutes. Fire is corrected between salvos, so a longer mission lands more accurately than a single round.

The AI commander

A commander turns loose groups into an operation: it takes objectives, defends what it holds, patrols the frontline, dispatches QRF, keeps a reserve and answers player requests.

Setting one up v0.4.137

In the Game Master editor

  1. In the Game Master content browser, place the DCO commander entity (E_AICommander).The mod registers it in the browser by itself — no config needed.
  2. Set its faction and mode, then place or select the objectives it should fight over.
  3. Select a group, open attributes and choose the commander in the DCO Commander dropdown. v0.4.138Only groups of the same faction as the commander appear.
  4. Assign vehicles the same way so the commander can use them for transport and support.
  5. To hand a group back, clear the dropdown — it returns to whatever it was doing before.

In the Workbench World Editor

  1. Drag Prefabs/Commander/Commander.et into the world and set its faction on AICommander_AlgorithmComponent.It spawns its own manager entity — you never place that yourself.
  2. Place Commander_Objective.et for each objective it should fight over.
  3. Either place groups and let the commander pick them up, or place a CommanderSpawner.et to spawn and hand over groups for it.

What Game Master exposes is listed in In Game Master; the prefabs and component attributes are in In the Workbench.

Give it a moment. By default the commander waits 60 s before its first decision cycle, then thinks every 30 s. Nothing will happen instantly.

Commander personality

Six sliders, all 0.5 by default, shape how a commander fights. Two commanders with the same units and objectives behave very differently once you move these. All six are in the DCO Commander attribute category.

SliderAt 0At 1
AggressionWaits for recon to confirm before committingAttacks immediately without waiting
AdaptabilitySlow to switch modes and reactVery responsive to changes
Risk takingRefuses to commit to objectives it has no intel onHappily attacks into the unknown
ResiliencePulls groups back earlyKeeps groups fighting much longer
PatienceQuickly reassigns groups stuck in a stalemateLets a stalled attack keep grinding
Combat focusIgnores small threats and stays on the objectiveChases contacts anywhere and reinforces easily

On top of that, mode sets the overall posture — offensive, defensive or balanced — and allowed tactics lets you restrict which of the thirteen plays it may choose. All are enabled by default.

Watching it think

A Game Master-only debug overlay draws what the commander believes: contact reports and their age, threat clusters, the frontline and the thresholds behind each decision. If a threat isn't being answered, the overlay shows which threshold it fell short of — that's usually faster than guessing.

In Game Master

Game Master reaches DCO through three places: the content browser for the entities you place, the command mode for DCO's own waypoints, and the DCO Settings window, which holds 839 settings across 16 folders. Everything you change there is live — it applies to the running session at once.

What you can place

Edit mode, content browser, filter by the DCO addon.

AI Commander ObjectiveAn area the commanders fight over. Give it a name, a radius (a town 100–150 m, a farm 40–60 m) and Controlled By for the side that starts with it. Leave that empty for a neutral objective.

Setting up a commander

Place it anywhere — position does not matter, it is a brain, not a unit. Select it and open DCO Settings.

Setting up an objective

Place it on what the fight is about — a town centre, a crossroads, a depot. The ring is drawn in the world so you can see the capture area.

RowSet it toWhy
NameRequiredMax 32 characters. It shows up in overlays, logs and briefings — an unnamed objective is miserable to debug.
Radius30 mTowns 100–150 m, a farm or crossroads 40–60 m. Units inside count toward control.
Controlled ByNeutralWho starts with it, at full control. Leave neutral and both sides race for it.
Base Value & Strategic Value50, ModerateHow badly commanders want it. High multiplies the base value by 1.67, Low halves it.
Handled ByAll CommandersWhich commanders may plan it at all. Only Checked and All Except Checked use the Commanders list below it.
Max Attack Groups / Defend Groups4 / 2How much force a commander commits here.
An objective nobody handles never changes hands. Control only advances while some commander is actively working on it. If you set Handled By to Only Checked and forget to tick anyone, the objective sits there forever — the start-up check reports it as handled_by=nobody.

Beyond the basics, an objective can also be a Type other than Capture — Defend, Hold until, Destroy or Recon, each with its own time limit and fail condition — and can sit in a Phase so it only opens once earlier objectives have fallen. Both phases and attack/defend roles are on by default.

Spawners and logistics

Place it behind your side's line. It builds groups near itself and hands them to a commander.

  1. Pick a Preset — that fills in the trigger for you.One-shot, Counterattack wave, Steady top-up, Commander on call, or Custom.
  2. Set Target Commander and Faction.Weakest of faction is the default, or name a commander. Empty faction means all.
  3. Leave the force pool empty unless you want specific groups.Empty means random groups from that faction's catalogue. Commander Chooses lets it pick AT against armour, recon for unclear contacts, motorized for distant objectives.
  4. Decide where the men come from with Take From Stock.None, men, or points from the commander's Reinforcement Stock. −1 is unlimited.
Pick the preset that matches the side. Counterattack wave fires when an objective your side holds is lost, so it only works for a defender with a trigger objective owned from the start. An attacker that owns nothing will never trigger it — give attackers Commander on call or Steady top-up.
Nothing spawning? The 300 m Safe Radius blocks it while any player or enemy is inside — that is what stops men appearing in front of players. Right-click › Spawn anyway skips it once. A spawner_idle line every two minutes says why a ready spawner is holding.

The logistics hub

Optional, but transport, MEDEVAC, extraction and resupply all gather here. It has one row of its own — Hub Faction, where Auto means the nearest commander.

A hub alone does nothing. It needs a transport team to work with. Any group of at most 3 men sitting in one unarmed vehicle with a driver and four free cargo seats becomes a transport team by itself; otherwise tick Dedicated Transport on a group and set its allowed jobs. Without a team every request waits five minutes and is cancelled. For resupply specifically the vehicle must be an ammo truck, and an arsenal or ammo truck at the hub lets low-ammo squads within 200 m restock on their own.

Using the two waypoints

  1. Switch to command mode and select the group.These are not in the content browser — they appear in the waypoint list, next to Move and Defend.
  2. Place Garrison (DCO) on the building or position to hold, or Do CQB (DCO) within 25 m of the building to clear.
  3. Its settings dialog opens by itself.The waypoint waits up to 60 s while you set it up, so take your time.
Garrison falls back gracefully: no building means field fighting positions, and failing that it behaves like a vanilla Defend. Do CQB is stricter — it needs a building with rooms on the navmesh, an outside door, and at least two men in the group.

What Game Master still does not reach

Two things, and that is the whole list. Everything else on this page is in the window — including the sector counts, staging distances, patrol standoff and first-think delay that used to be prefab-only.

SettingDefaultWhy it matters
Commander think interval30 sThe main decision cycle and the biggest commander cost. Not the Think Interval under Commander: Threat Response — that one is threat evaluation and defaults to 45 s. The shipped US_Commander.et raises the real one to 150 s, so a Workbench mission is already slower than the script default.
Key defend positions2How many groups go to evaluated key positions rather than defending loosely.
Game Master changes are not saved. They last for the session. To keep them, use Save to server config if your server allows it (gmSaveConfig: gm, admin or off — admin by default), save a settings preset, or set the value in the Workbench.

In the Workbench

Everything the Game Master can do live, a mission maker can bake into the scenario instead — so the mission ships with its own AI settings and nobody has to touch a slider at runtime.

The order that works

  1. Place CommanderManager.et once, anywhere in the world.Everything else registers with it. Skip this and nothing runs.
  2. Place one commander per AI side and set Faction Key and Commander UID on AICommander_AlgorithmComponent.Category Commander 01. The UID is how spawners and objectives refer to it.
  3. Place the objectives and give each a name, a radius and an initial owner.Initial Owner Commander UID is the Workbench name for what Game Master calls Controlled By.
  4. Place the groups, then tick Auto Assign on each.Optionally set Dedicated Co to a commander UID; empty means a random commander of the same faction. It retries for a minute, then gives up with a log line.
  5. Optional: a spawner per side, a logistics hub with a transport team, and a mortar plus a crew of up to four within 40 m.
Destroy objectives are Workbench-only. The Destroy Targets list takes entity names, which Game Master has no way to enter. Everything else about objectives works in both.
Two prefabs you never place. CommanderManager.et is spawned by the Game Master commander (but not the Workbench one, see above), and Prefabs/DCO/DCO_FactionSettings.et is spawned by DCO itself — one per faction that has a block in the server config.

Getting groups under a commander

A group built on Prefabs/AI/Groups/Group_Base.et already carries the DCO group components. What it will not do is join a commander by itself.

WhereHow

Globals are copied at spawn. A unit takes the global values when it spawns, in either editor. Changing a global in Game Master does not retrofit AI already on the map — respawn it, or set the per-unit value.
Server owners get a third route that overrides neither editor confusingly: the config folder is read once at startup, and can be switched off so the editor values stand.

Building a mission, step by step

Four missions, each one adding a layer to the one before it. The values below are the ones the shipped example missions actually use, so you can follow along or just copy them.

You don't have to build these by hand. They ship as an optional addon, DCO Examples, with four worlds and four scenario entries. Load it alongside DCO_AI and they appear in the scenario list; open its project in the Workbench to take the worlds apart. Each world is a sub-scene of the vanilla GM_Arland, so it reuses that map's navmesh.

1. The smallest mission that works

Two commanders, three objectives, four squads. Nothing else. If this runs, your setup is sound and everything later is an addition.

  1. Place one CommanderManager.Workbench only — the Game Master commander brings its own.
  2. Place two commanders and name them.The example uses Viking (US) and Volga (USSR). Faction and name are the two rows you cannot skip.
  3. Place three objectives and decide who starts with each.A village held by Volga, a farm left neutral in the middle, a crossroads held by Viking. The neutral one in the middle is what makes them meet.
  4. Place two squads per side, near their own commander.Workbench: tick Auto Assign and set Dedicated Co to that commander's name. Game Master: set the group's Commander attribute.
  5. Unpause and press Ctrl + I to watch the plan.

What a healthy start looks like

TimeWhat you should see
T+7 sEvery squad has joined its commander. If one hasn't, its Auto Assign is off or its faction doesn't match.
T+20 sThe start-up check runs. setup_check_done warn=0 means the scene is fine; anything else names the problem.
T+30 sObjectives that failed the first navmesh test are re-checked, for tiles that hadn't loaded yet.
T+65 sThe first orders go out. Before this the commander is simply waiting out its first-think delay — it is not broken.

2. Give it a way to end

The first mission runs forever. An operation and a win condition turn it into something with a shape.

  1. On the objective you want the fight to be about, turn on Operation Primary.The commander then plans the whole operation around it instead of treating all three as separate jobs.
  2. Tick the other two under Linked Secondaries.Or leave that empty and let Secondary Radius (600 m) pick up whatever is nearby.
  3. Add DCO_VictoryComponent and turn on Hold All Primary.It works on any entity, so you can put it on a spare one and leave the game mode untouched.

The commander now attacks in order rather than all at once — in the example, checkpoint first, then the secondary, then the primary. The log shows op_plan with the primary named, and victory_init confirming which rules are live.

3. Defenders in buildings, attackers with reserves

Now the two sides stop being symmetrical.

  1. Give the defender a squad with a Garrison (DCO) waypoint on the objective.In the Workbench this goes under the group's Static Waypoints. The squad scans the village and takes window positions.
  2. Give the attacking commander a Reinforcement Stock — the example uses 60 men.That is the pool the spawner draws from, and what the defender has to bleed dry.
  3. Place a spawner behind the attacker's line, preset Commander on call.Target that commander by name, Take From Stock = Men, and a force pool of a rifle squad (weight 2) and a fire team (weight 1).
  4. Lower the spawner's Safe Radius if your map is tight.The example uses 150 m instead of the default 300 m, and a 60 s cooldown.
Match the preset to the side. Commander on call for an attacker, because the commander asks when it is worn down. Counterattack wave only fires when an objective your side holds is lost — on an attacker that owns nothing, it can never trigger, and DCO will tell you so with spawner_trigger_none.

4. The full template

Everything above plus logistics, in five layers named in the order you build them.

LayerContains
01_CommandersThe manager, plus Viking (US) and Volga (USSR) with 60 stock each.
02_ObjectivesPrimary with its two linked secondaries — all three held by the defender at start.
03_GroupsDefender: a garrison squad and one free squad. Attacker: two rifle squads and three fire teams. All on Auto Assign.
04_Spawner_LogisticsAttacker on Commander on call, defender on Steady top-up, and a logistics hub per side.
05_VictoryHold All Primary and Enemy Stock Empty together — the attacker wins by taking the primary, the defender by bleeding the attacker dry.
Two things the template leaves for you. A hub needs an actual transport team and an ammo source to do anything (see spawners and logistics), and a Do CQB waypoint placed statically in the Workbench is not picked up — give it at runtime or from Game Master.

When it doesn't work

Almost every first-mission problem is one of these.

SymptomCauseFix
Commander does nothing at allNo CommanderManager in a Workbench mission.Place one, or use the Game Master prefabs which bring their own.
Nothing happens for the first minuteNormal. First think delay, then the think interval — and the shipped commander prefab uses 150 s.Lower both for a small test mission.
Groups never joinAuto Assign off, or the commander's faction is empty or different.Tick Auto Assign, set the faction. Look for faction=EMPTY.
A side doesn't start with its objectivesInitial owner left empty.Set Controlled By (Workbench: Initial Owner Commander UID).
An objective never changes handsNo commander is planning it, or Allowed Factions excludes the attacker.Check Handled By and the objective's base value.
AI doesn't move at allNo navmesh, or the objective sits off it.Generate the soldier navmesh. Watch for navmesh=none.
Do CQB ends immediatelyNo building within 25 m, no outside door or rooms on the navmesh, or fewer than two men.Move the waypoint, raise the target radius, use a bigger group.
Spawner never spawnsA player or enemy inside the safe radius, the faction AI cap, an empty stock, or the cooldown.Check spawner_defer, or right-click › Spawn anyway.
A config value does nothingA typo in the key, or the old default still sitting in the file.Check the key reference; delete the key to take the new default.
Read the start-up check first. Twenty seconds in, DCO writes one block of setup_check lines and tells every Game Master online if something is wrong. It catches the missing faction, the missing UID, the objective nobody handles and the missing navmesh — which between them cover most of the table above.

Garrisons Dev build

Garrisoning puts a group into buildings by slot — windows, rooftops, balconies, doorways — instead of milling around inside a room.

Setting it up

  1. Select a group in commanding mode and place the Garrison (DCO) waypoint on the building or position you want held.In the Workbench, add Prefabs/AI/Waypoints/AIWaypoint_Garrison.et as a waypoint child of the group instead.
  2. Pick the mode.Hold never leaves the building · Defend allows a small flank team outside · Release garrisons until a trigger frees the group.
  3. Adjust how it spreads and where it looks, using the settings below.

Clearing a building: Do CQB

The second DCO waypoint. The group clears the building nearest the waypoint, room by room, and you never place an indoor navigation graph — DCO builds one from the soldier navmesh at runtime.

SettingDefaultEffect
CQB target radius25 mHow far from the waypoint to look for the building. The nearest one wins.
CQB attempts2Tries before giving up and moving on, 15 s apart.
Hold after clearOffStay inside afterwards. The waypoint then never completes.
The plan fails when the building has no rooms on the navmesh, no outside door, or the group has fewer than two men. A building another friendly group is already clearing is skipped.
A debug option draws the slots in use — red windows, orange roof, blue doors, grey interior — which makes it obvious when a building offers fewer firing positions than you expected.
By default one building belongs to one group. If you want several groups sharing a large structure, turn on building sharing in DCO Global Combat.

Per unit

Select a single soldier in the editor to override skill, personality, perception, accuracy, suppression sensitivity, dodging, grenade usage, unlimited ammo and hold position. Useful for making one sniper deadly without touching the rest of the map.

Personality

Every soldier gets one of four personalities at spawn, drawn from weights you control. The defaults make most soldiers steady, with a few outliers:

PersonalityDefault weightBehaviour
Measured (standard)80Baseline: returns fire after about 5 s of being pinned.
Cautious7Stays down about 8 s, looks harder for good cover, saves ammo earlier.
Aggressive12Back in the fight in about 3.5 s, holds firing positions stubbornly.
Reckless3About 2 s, rarely takes cover, refuses to go prone, dodges least.

Raise the reckless and aggressive weights for fanatical insurgents; raise cautious for green conscripts who hug cover.

Common setups

Starting points, not rules — every value here is in the tables above.

Tougher, more aggressive enemies

  • Raise aggressive and reckless personality weights, lower cautious
  • Raise global skill and lower aim improvement time so they zero in faster
  • Superior ratio down to about 1.5 so they push on thinner odds
  • Grenade usage up to 0.8
  • Commander aggression and risk taking to 0.8, reserve down to 10%

A defended town

  • Garrison waypoints on the key buildings, mode Defend so flank teams can react
  • Fill ratio around 0.5 to spread over more buildings
  • Threat weight near 1 if you know which way the attack comes, lower if you don't
  • Release triggers on casualties so the garrison doesn't die in place pointlessly
  • Commander mode defensive, sectors raised for a larger perimeter

Close-terrain infantry fight

  • Squad engagement distance, infantry down to 300–500 m so they don't snipe across the map
  • CQB on, stack wait lower for faster entries
  • Smoke usage up so movement gets screened
  • Cover preference set to indoor

Night operations

  • Keep night behaviour and light discipline on
  • Engagement multiplier at 0.4–0.6 for genuinely short night engagements
  • Players: call illumination before assaulting
  • Expect the commander to illuminate before its own attacks

Large PvE server on a budget

  • Turn on LOD mode for commander-owned groups
  • Commander think interval up to 45–60 s
  • Contact report scan interval up, max reports per scan down
  • Cap AI per faction with the Commander Spawner limit
  • Measure with the benchmark logger before and after

For server owners

Server settings live in a folder of JSON files in the profile folder, so you can tune DCO without opening the editor or repacking anything.

The config folder v0.4.144

This is the short version. Every key, with its shipped value and range, plus ready-to-copy templates, is on the server config page.

  1. Start the server once with DCO_AI loaded.It writes $profile:DCO/Config/ — Core.json, Soldiers.json, Squads.json, Commander.json, Vehicles.json, plus Advanced/ and Factions/.
  2. Edit the values you care about and restart.
  3. After a mod update that adds settings, the old file is copied to .bak and only the missing keys are appended — your values are preserved.

Reading the file can be switched off entirely, in which case the editor values are used as-is. Radio lines and default UI settings have their own files (DCO_Radio.json, DCO_UI.json).

Coming from a single file? An old DCO_GlobalConfig.json is split into the folder automatically on first start, every value kept, and renamed to .migrated.bak. Full key reference and ready-made templates: server config.
Don't edit while the server runs. The config is read at startup; changes made to a running server are overwritten on the next write.

Troubleshooting

SymptomCheck

Commander dropdown shows only "None"No commander exists for that faction yet, or the roster hasn't replicated to this machine. Place a commander of the same faction as the group.
